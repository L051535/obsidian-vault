# Electrical Safety Awareness Training

**Type:** Training Course (NALO)
**Supersedes:** —

## Overview

Working around electricity and electrical equipment poses hazards for all employees. In this course, learners will:
- Be able to identify hazards and safe work practices in the work area.
- Know which tasks they may and may not perform.

## Classifications of Electrical Workers

There are 2 classifications of electrical workers:
- **Unqualified Affected Persons**
- **Qualified Electrical Workers (QEW)**

### Unqualified Affected Person

An Unqualified Affected Person is any employee who works around electrical equipment but does not meet the qualifications to be a Qualified Electrical Worker. Tasks/activities involving electrical equipment that an Unqualified Affected Person may perform are limited to those covered below.

**Tasks an Unqualified Affected Person MAY perform:**
- Operation of 120-volt power switches.
- Operation of Local start/stop switches.
- Operation of Emergency-stop buttons.
- Plug and unplug 120 volt and 480-volt receptacles, agitator cords, and battery charger cords.
- Access a control panel to operate disconnects.
- Reset (one time only) any reset button that is accessible without opening an electrical panel.

> [!note]
> An Unqualified Affected Person MAY NOT remove faceplates or otherwise open control panels. Additional resets, after one reset, must be performed by a qualified electrical worker.

**Tasks an Unqualified Affected Person MAY NOT perform:**

| May Not Perform | May Not Perform |
|---|---|
| Open junction boxes | Apply hard wire jumpers |
| Operate any circuit breaker | Reset Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS) overloads |
| Work on, or near exposed, energized parts | Replace blown fuses |
| Work on parts that are de-energized, but not locked out/tagged out | Work on load center gear in electrical switch rooms |
| Reset electrical equipment that requires a QEW after 1 attempt | Work on electrical circuits over 480-volts |
| Remove electrical panel guards | Reset, close, open, or trip 480-volt load center feed breakers |
| Enter any electrical vaults/rooms | Establish temporary wiring |
| Perform electrical work on 24-, 120-, or 480-volt electrical equipment | Perform electrical maintenance and repair |
| Perform energized electrical work on a component of an electrical power system within reaching distance of exposed energized parts | Operation of 480-volt equipment disconnects |
| Open an electrical panel door (to view panel schedule) | |

> [!warning]
> Only High Voltage Electrical Workers may reset, open, close, or trip 480-volt load center feed breakers.

### Qualified Electrical Worker

A Qualified Electrical Worker is an employee who has completed required QEW training per established global/local curriculum (including documented supervisory approval as outlined in local training course) AND has met at least one of the following:
- Four-year electrical engineering degree.
- Completion of a four-year apprenticeship.
- Two-year technical degree within an electrical discipline, plus related experience.
- Lilly shop/craft training and advancement through job classifications.
- Supervisor has certified the employee is qualified based on experience and demonstrated ability to safely perform QEW tasks.

## Working Safely Around Electricity — General Guidelines

Covers key guidelines for electrical equipment, cords/cables/wires, and plugs/extension cords/power strips:
- Avoid wearing jewelry or metal objects when working with electricity.
- Use safety features like three-pronged plugs, double-insulated tools, and safety switches.

### Guidelines for Electrical Equipment

- Ensure electrical equipment is maintained properly: regularly inspect tools, cords, grounds, and accessories; check for loose/deformed/missing parts; inspect for damaged jackets or insulation.
- Ensure machine guards are in place.
- Follow Lockout/Tagout procedures.
- Ensure damaged electrical equipment is removed from use.
- Do not modify electrical equipment from the manufacturer's original design.
- Do not remove the grounding prong from plugs.
- Do not modify or repair broken antennas with coat hangers, wires, etc. — remove broken antennas from service.
- Do not attempt to repair electrical equipment with tape, glue, or other methods.

### Guidelines for Cords, Cables and Wires

- Use only UL certified cords, rated for the tool/equipment in use. (If cord rating information is not legible, remove the cord from service.)
- Do not staple or otherwise fasten a cord in a way that damages its outer jacket.
- Keep electric cables and cords free from pinch points or kinks.
- Do not raise, lower, or carry equipment by its cord, or unplug equipment by pulling on its cord.
- Notify supervision or maintenance if signs of damage or deterioration are identified.

### Guidelines for Extension Cords

- Do not use extension cords on a permanent basis — may be used as temporary wiring (less than 30 days, per Lilly requirements) if compliant with the National Electric Code.
- When portable tools are used with an extension cord, a GFCI must be used.
- Inspect for damage prior to use.

### Guidelines for Outlets and Surge Protectors / Power Strips

- Never overload any outlet — more than two plugs per double outlet is considered overloaded.
- Surge protectors/power strips can easily be overloaded and should only be used for low-wattage equipment (calculators, computers, desktop staplers, small fans, etc.). Never for laser printers, appliances, copiers, or pedestal fans.

## Inspection of Electrical Equipment Prior to Use

An initial inspection is required for electrical equipment arriving at a Lilly site, to assure the equipment meets UL/FM or equivalent standards, has no apparent defects or broken/worn parts, and is properly grounded as required. Equipment issued by Lilly is inspected by a Lilly QEW or QEW agent prior to issuance.

Non-Lilly provided electrical equipment (e.g., an iPod, pencil sharpener) no longer requires an electrical inspection sticker, but radios, fans, and other heat-generating equipment still require inspection. Prior to bringing equipment on site, employees ensure it is certified by a nationally recognized testing lab (e.g., UL) and free of visual defects (contact maintenance supervision if in question).

Employee-provided equipment must also be routinely inspected by the user for cord damage or obvious hazards — remove from service immediately if found.

> [!warning]
> Heat generating equipment may only be kept in designated break areas. The following are no longer permitted: portable heaters, coffee cup warmers, heating pads, foot warmers, electric blankets.

The following appliances are only permitted in Approved break areas: Coffee Pots, Microwaves, Toasters, Refrigerators, Toaster Ovens (special requirements), Coffee Cup Warmers.

> [!note]
> The maintenance and housekeeping of these units will continue to be monitored.

## Access to Electrical Panels

Never block access to electrical panels such as circuit breaker panels, switchboards, load centers, VFD Cabinets, motor control centers, combination starters, and control panels. Many areas have signs, bollards, and/or floor markings in front of electrical panels. Equipment such as carts, pallets, skids, chairs, trash receptacles, or PITs must never be placed in front of electrical panels, even temporarily — a minimum of 36" of clearance should be allowed.

## Electrical Safety Awareness — Examples

- Cords not aligned properly in a cable tray caused a pinch point and cord damage, creating potential for shock from an electric short or arc.
- Access to electrical panels blocked despite signage — hazardous to personnel, equipment, and facilities.

## Course Summary

To be safe around electrical equipment, every employee must:
- Perform ONLY the tasks that they are qualified to do.
- Know the hazards and follow safe work practices.
- Be aware of your environment and report any unusual situations or conditions.
