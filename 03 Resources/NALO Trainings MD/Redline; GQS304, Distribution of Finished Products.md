# Redline: GQS304 — Distribution of Finished Products

**Type:** Global Quality Standard (redline, v12 → v13)
**Approved by:** Melissa Seymour, Quality Approver (25 Mar 2026)
**Previous approver (v12):** Johna Norton, EVP Global Quality

> [!warning] This is a redline
> This note captures the **v13** (Pending Effective 01-Aug-2026) content of GQS304, with the key changes from v12 (effective 01-Apr-2023) called out inline. Verify current effective status in QualityDocs before relying on this for compliance decisions.

## Summary of Changes (v12 → v13)

1. **Consignment pharmacies** added throughout as a distribution channel/next legal owner (Purpose, Scope, Figure 1 product flow, Section 6.1 risk assessment, Section 6.3 shipping requirements) — reflects the shift in shipping configuration from pallet to individual unit mid-distribution path.
2. **Deviation management references** updated from `GQS104 – Deviation Management` to `GSOP-104-8, Quality Deviation Management Overview` throughout (Sections 3.3, 3.5, 5.1.4, 8.3, 9.4.3, 9.5).
3. **Product theft reporting** changed: Section 9.4.1 now requires a **deviation** (not a Notification to Management) for suspected theft, referencing GSOP-104-8 instead of the retired LQP-101-1 NTM process.
4. **Suspect/illegitimate product notification** (Section 9.1.2): removed the requirement to notify management via NTM (GSOP-101-3 no longer requires it for suspect product) — language now just requires notification "according to" the deviation process.
5. **Shipping label requirements** (Section 7.3.4) clarified: labels must show **SAP temperature conditions and additional instructions, or equivalent** (previously just "storage conditions") — see TR 40647726.
6. **LQS303** renamed from "Controlled Drugs" to "**Controlled Substances**" and Lilly-Designated Special Security Substances (Sections 4.2.3, 6.3.6).
7. **LQS135** renamed from "Product Withdrawal" to "**Market Withdrawal**" (Section 9.3).
8. Performance monitoring (Section 12.1.1, 12.1.3) now explicitly includes **product theft or loss** among complaint/trend categories monitored.
9. Copyright year updated to 2026; document approver changed to Melissa Seymour.

## Purpose

Distribution is a key interface between Lilly and the customer. Effective distribution controls ensure the right product reaches the right customer under the right conditions while maintaining product quality. Applies to distribution of finished products, including physician samples and product donations — covering receipt, storage, shipment preparation, and shipping to the next legal owner (e.g., wholesalers, pharmacies, **consignment pharmacies**, patients).

## Scope

Covers activities from receipt of finished products (production, other Lilly DCs, contract manufacturers, joint ventures) through distribution to affiliates, sales reps (physician samples), other Lilly DCs, wholesalers, **consignment pharmacies**, and the customer.

Pertains to: storage, distribution operations; finished products, medical devices, drug/device combination products. Throughout the standard, "product" includes finished products, medical devices, and drug/device combination products.

## 1.0 Distribution Overview

Figure 1 (Distribution Product Flow) — updated to add a **Consignment Pharmacy** node between "Other Distribution Center" and "Customer" in the outbound flow.

### 1.1 Authorization

- All Lilly distribution sites must be licensed/authorized per local regulations.
- Product sold/distributed only to licensed/authorized persons/entities.
- EU wholesale distributors must designate a **Responsible Person (RP)** meeting Member State legislation; outside the EU, the site quality leader for distribution holds this responsibility.
- Quality unit ensures distribution only to approved markets consistent with batch release (GQS102).
- Only approved contracted distributors/transport providers (GQS307) and authorized wholesalers may be used. US: a current, quality-unit-approved list of wholesale distributors (with authorized products, or a statement of blanket authorization) must be maintained.

### 1.2 System

Must address, at minimum: receipt/storage, order processing, picking/packing, country-specific distribution designation, shipping, traceability/documentation/reconciliation, and complaints/recalls/returns/withdrawals/imports/exports/donations.

- Product shipped under conditions per storage label/instructions for use/stability data. Acceptable out-of-condition time periods (staging, load/unload) must be defined and quality-unit approved.
  - *Stability Budget documents* may define acceptable Time Out of Refrigeration (TOR) for refrigerated parenterals.
- Sites must maintain a complete ownership-traceability record through the supply chain to the entity selling/supplying to the patient/agent.
- Sites must document delivery of special-condition products in a risk assessment, including temperature-change controls during transport.
- Sites must have a system (physical or validated electronic) to segregate products when required.

### 1.3 Facilities

- Designed to minimize risk of damage, mix-ups, substitutions (GQS202).
- Designed/equipped/operated to meet security requirements and minimize pest infestation (GQS202).
- Receiving/shipping bays protected from weather; receiving area separate from storage.
- Reception areas allow container cleaning before storage where necessary.

### 1.4 General Distribution Requirements

- Cold chain distribution: use specialized cold-chain distributors/transport providers when available.
- Quality agreements required for contracted distributors/transport providers per GQS307 (must cover sub-contracting provisions; Lilly does not need direct agreements with sub-contractors).
- **Groupage** (consolidating Lilly product with other companies' goods) permitted only if: non-hazardous/non-perishable; approved in the distributor's quality agreement; temperature/transit conditions suitable for all products (e.g., 2-8°C not grouped with frozen); transport provider oversight in place; special security/controlled substances not identifiable as such within the groupage.
- Rationale for shipping cold chain + CRT together under refrigeration (e.g., 2-8°C) must be documented/quality-unit approved; not permitted where restricted by DTP or local regulations.
- Products handled/stored to prevent spillage, breakage, contamination, mix-ups.

## 2.0 Personnel

### 2.1 Responsible Person Responsibilities

Quality function ensures GDP compliance. RP responsibilities include: quality management system oversight, authorized-activity/record accuracy, training programs, recall coordination, complaint handling, supplier/customer approval, subcontracted-activity approval, self-inspections, delegated-duty records, disposition decisions for returned/rejected/recalled/suspect product, saleable-stock return approval, and national-law compliance.

### 2.2 Training

- All distribution personnel trained on GDP requirements per LQS107 before starting tasks.
- Training on supply chain controls to avoid suspect product entering the market.
- Specific training for hazardous/radioactive products, abuse-risk products (narcotics), temperature-specific products.
- Insulated-box assembly personnel trained on relevant procedures.
- Initial + continuing training per formal program; RP maintains GDP competence via regular training.

### 2.3 Personnel Hygiene

Procedures covering health, hygiene, clothing must be established/observed.

## 3.0 Receipt

### 3.1 Receipts from Production

Finished product from production must be segregated/quarantined until approved/released for transfer/shipment.

### 3.2 Shipment Receipt

Each incoming shipment examined (by quality-unit-authorized personnel) for: labeling, correct shipping (durations, qualified container, temp-controlled vehicle), order/material/delivery-note agreement, container integrity, temperature monitoring data acceptability, proof of batch release/QP certification (GQS102), overt safety features present/correct, and heat-treated wooden pallets free of TBPs/TBAs (**heat treatment is the only acceptable pest control method for wood pallets**). Receipt verification must be documented.

Finished products quarantined post-receipt (physical segregation, procedural controls/status labels, or validated computer system) until approved. European sites must segregate non-EU-country receipts not intended for the European market.

### 3.3 Approval for Distribution

- Only the quality unit approves incoming finished product for distribution — verifying receipt documentation, country-specific status, and computer system data entry.
- Product with potential quality impact must be quarantined and reported to the quality unit, evaluated per **GSOP-104-8, Quality Deviation Management Overview** *(was: GQS104 – Deviation Management)*.
- Suspect product incidents communicated per GQS302; suspect product physically segregated in a dedicated area, evaluated per GSOP-104-8.

### 3.4 Incoming Finished Product Records

Must include: date/quantity received, PO/sales order, product name, batch/control number and expiration date, dosage form/strength description, supplier name/address, approval/rejection status, special handling instructions, temperature data.

### 3.5 Unforeseen Temperature Excursions

Evaluated per **GSOP-104-8** using the DTP; shipment/evaluation responsibility described in an inter-site quality agreement.

## 4.0 Storage

### 4.1 Containers

Cleaned when necessary prior to storage.

### 4.2 Storage Areas

- Drums/bags/boxes stored off the floor (e.g., on pallets), suitably spaced for cleaning/inspection or periodic stock rotation (e.g., pallets untouched 6+ months).
- Documentation verifying pest-control treatment of Lilly-purchased cardboard/wooden pallets; wooden pallets must be heat-treated and free of TBPs/TBAs (heat treatment only acceptable method); process to control pallet movement required.
- Controlled/special security substances stored in safe, dedicated, secure areas per **LQS303 – Controlled Substances** *(renamed from "Drugs")* and Lilly-Designated Special Security Substances. Highly active (e.g., flammable) products per HSE-STD-600.

### 4.3 Storage Conditions

- Storage conditions defined per specification (GQS108); areas protect from light/heat/cold extremes per spec/registration commitments and official compendia (USP/JP/EP).
- Special conditions established per GQS202, qualified per GQS201; temperature mapping at locations most likely to see extremes (near lighting, doors, vents).
- Monitored storage areas with alarm systems for temperature/humidity; quality unit reviews excursion/alarm records; alarm/monitoring devices maintained per GQS215.

### 4.4 Segregated Storage Areas

Physical/electronic segregation for near-expiry (SAP adjusted date), expired, or quarantined product. Market-withdrawn material must be physically segregated; EU expired product must also be physically segregated. Physical areas clearly marked, restricted, secured on all sides.

## 5.0 Inventory Control

### 5.1 Inventory Management

- Individual inventory record per finished product (traceability, batch status, reconciliation).
- **FEFO** (first expired, first out) process required unless customer agreement specifies otherwise (e.g., 75% shelf life remaining per WHO guidance); FEFO changes must be temporary/documented/quality-approved.
- Expired product segregated/rejected.
- Inventory reconciliation (cycle count) at least annually, and as part of discrepancy investigations. Tolerance levels set by site ops + quality unit; out-of-tolerance adjustments require quality approval; discrepancies evaluated per **GSOP-104-8**.
  - Unaccounted-for serialized product must be de-aggregated from the parent (serial number not decommissioned; record updated to "unaccounted for" state).
- All finished products must be locatable within **48 hours**.

### 5.2 Quarantine Status

System required for quarantine during investigation of potentially-impacted product in Lilly custody.

Controls: segregation, clear status identification, area/personnel advisement, quality-unit-only release, no use until release (release authority commensurate with batch-release authority), and quarantine records (deviation reference, releasing rep, release date).

Released product may be returned to stock, reprocessed (per GQS401), or destroyed.

### 5.3 De-Aggregation of Serial Numbers

Units picked from a serialized case must have their serial numbers de-aggregated from the case.

### 5.4 Unaccounted for Serialized Product

Must be decommissioned once the return-refund time period expires (≤1 year post-expiry, unless local regulations require otherwise).

## 6.0 Establishing Shipping Requirements

### 6.1 Distribution Risk Assessment

Each site evaluates: country regulatory requirements, product temperature requirements, other hazards (chemical, shock, vibration), transport environment, and **(v13 addition)** the complete distribution pathway from Lilly to the final point of the chain per Figure 1 — including intermittent stops and/or shipping configuration changes (e.g., **consignment pharmacies**, pallet-to-individual-unit switches).

### 6.2 Distribution Temperature Profiles

- DTP developed per drug product from stability studies (created by TS/MS, approved by quality unit); defines time/temperature ranges for safe transport.
- Stability Budget document defines acceptable TOR during routine assembly/packaging/distribution, plus the DTP allowance.
- Without a DTP (e.g., acquired product), stability data determines shipping conditions.

### 6.3 Shipping Requirements

Defined per product/family, including: temperature range/time limits (aligned to stability data/Stability Budget), acceptable transport methods, external temperature conditions, max shipment duration, packaging requirements (qualified containers, qualified vehicles, packing materials like ice/cool/gel packs/loadbox, external protection, and **(v13 addition)** consideration of all shipping configurations including pallet-to-individual-unit switches mid-distribution), temperature monitoring requirements, and local regulatory requirements.

Wooden pallets used for shipment must be heat-treated, free of TBPs/TBAs.

**Transportation method/equipment selection** based on: stability impact of time/temperature, destination, route, required transport conditions, regional climate/seasonal variation, delivery schedules, local regulations.

**Temperature-sensitive products** shipped via temperature-controlled vehicle, qualified containers, or monitoring (where route conditions don't require added protection). Exceptions require documentation/rationale and approval by the global quality leader (frozen/refrigerated) or site/regional quality leader (CRT).

**Additional requirements:** maintain product identity; protect against spillage/breakage; prevent contamination (unclean vehicles, other product, pests); maintain temperature limits; security measures against tampering/unauthorized access (e.g., trailer seals).

Special security/controlled substances transported in safe/secure containers/vehicles per **LQS303 – Controlled Substances**; highly active material per HSE-STD-600.

## 7.0 Order Preparation

### 7.1 Distribution Orders

- Shipment order required before distribution.
- Quality-unit-approved systems ensure correct order/country/labeling assignment.
- Orders prepared/shipped per defined shipping requirements.

### 7.2 Order Picking Process

Verification (validated computer system or second-person) of: item code, batch/control number, expiration date, storage location, quantity, product description, approval status.

### 7.3 Order Packing Process

- Containers (cardboard cartons, styrofoam, etc.) must not adversely affect product quality and must protect from external conditions.
- Correct container/packing material (ice/cool/gel packs) per temperature requirements, destination, transport method, shipping time; packaging material must not directly contact product.
- System to control cool pack reuse (avoid incompletely cooled packs); frozen/chilled ice packs physically separated.
- **(v13 change)** Shipping label/documentation must state **SAP temperature conditions and additional instructions, or equivalent** *(previously: "storage conditions" if special transport/storage conditions required)* — see TR 40647726.
- Shipping labels must remain affixed/legible during shipping.
- Order packed-correctly verification system required; orders reconciled (order vs. actual quantity) prior to shipment; unreconciled orders held pending investigation.

## 8.0 Shipping

### 8.1 Shipping and Transportation

Shipment record must include: product name, description (dosage form, strength), batch number, consignee name/address, date/quantity shipped, transportation company.

### 8.2 Monitoring

Temperature monitoring used during shipping for temperature-sensitive products remaining in Lilly control, where required/per local regulations. Monitoring devices maintained per GQS215; device locations recorded in shipping documentation.

### 8.3 Unforeseen Temperature Excursions During Shipment

Evaluated per **GSOP-104-8** using the DTP; responsibility described in a quality agreement.

## 9.0 Returns, Recalls, and Market Withdrawals

### 9.1 General

- Returned/impounded/recalled/market-withdrawn product segregated per Section 3.0.
- Suspect product in the distribution network immediately quarantined, labeled "not for sale" (GQS302 for handling). **(v13 change)** Management notification of confirmed illegitimate/falsified product is no longer required via NTM — GSOP-101-3 no longer mandates NTM for suspect product *(previously: LQP-101-1/GSOP-101-3 Quality Notification to Management)*.

### 9.2 Returned Product

Records: product name, consignee name/address, batch number/quantity returned, reason, disposition, date of final release/rejection.

Held/identified as returned; doubt about storage/shipping conditions or product/container/labeling condition generally means **destruction**; reprocessing (relabeling/repackaging) per GQS401 if investigation supports it; destruction documented/quality-approved. Quarantined until quality decision per GQS102.

### 9.3 Recalls and Market Withdrawals

Managed per GQS131 (Recalls) and **LQS135 – Market Withdrawal** *(renamed from "Product Withdrawal")*.

### 9.4 Stolen or Lost Product

- **(v13 change)** A **deviation** must be issued for suspected theft within/outside Lilly, per **GSOP-104-8, Quality Deviation Management Overview** *(previously: a notification to management per LQP-101-1 NTM — no longer used for product theft)*.
- Recovered product (after loss of control) must be quarantined.
- Impact on product quality/supply chain evaluated per GSOP-104-8.
- Global quality leader approves any return of recovered product to saleable stock; **stolen product cannot be returned to saleable stock for the European market**.

### 9.5 Product Recovery (Salvaging)

Product subjected to improper storage (temperature/humidity/smoke/fumes/pressure/age/radiation extremes from disasters, fires, accidents, equipment failure) must not be used. Evaluate per GSOP-104-8 when in doubt; global quality leader approves any decision to use.

### 9.6 Returning Product to Saleable Stock

Placed to preserve FEFO operation.

### 9.7 Product Destruction

Destruction documented/quality-unit-approved, per international/national/local handling/transport/disposal requirements. Destroyed serialized product status updated (decommissioned) with documented reason.

## 10.0 Import and Export

- System ensures import/export regulation and security compliance.
- Authorized quality rep/QP approves each batch/partial batch before release (GQS102); if exported onward from intended destination, the releasing rep/QP must be informed.
- For batches released under the same marketing authorization but split, the rep/QP ensures import/export records are kept and all parts traceable.
- Distributors/importers cooperate with manufacturers/authorized reps for traceability.

## 11.0 Product Donations

- Based on expressed need/consent; each affiliate has a documented process and designated responsible person.
- Follow destination-country rules/regulations.
- Donated drug presentation/strength/formulation should resemble what's commonly used in the recipient country.
- ≥6 months expiration dating required; <6 months requires global quality leader + country managing director approval.
- Must comply with country-of-manufacture specification and meet quality standards in both donor/recipient countries.
- Must not be donated unless suitable for sale.

## 12.0 Performance Monitoring and Evaluation

- System to evaluate/respond to distribution complaints: overages, shortages, late shipments, shipment damage, incorrect deliveries, **and (v13 addition) product theft or loss**.
- Site quality leader + GQAAC informed of any supply disruption risking permanent/temporary drug shortage, per LQP-112-7 (Global Product Shortage and Discontinuation Reporting to Regulatory Authorities).
- Distribution management reviews reconciliation results, complaint trends, unforeseen temperature excursions, **and (v13 addition) product theft or loss trends** as part of management review per GQS101.
