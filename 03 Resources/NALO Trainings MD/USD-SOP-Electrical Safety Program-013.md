# Electrical Safety Program

**Type:** Health and Safety Procedure
**Areas Involved:** North American Logistics Operations (NALO) Supervision and Management (includes Quality Assurance), and NALO Engineering and Maintenance

## Purpose

To prevent injury to all personnel who work with or near electrical hazards by promoting an electrically safe workplace free from unauthorized exposure to electrical hazards for all employees and contractors. This program is based on the referenced documents; all applicable elements of those references are part of this program. Where local requirements differ from these requirements and do not compromise safety, modifications should be made to reflect those differences.

## Scope

The Electrical Safety program is a minimum standard applying to all manufacturing, research and development, sales and marketing, and distribution sites. Scope of implementation may vary by site functionality and size. US distribution centers conform to the Corporation's electrical safety requirements and to local/country jurisdiction — where regulations differ, the more stringent must be followed.

> [!note]
> **Approach boundary distances (NFPA 70E):** If the actual arc flash boundary distance has not been posted or determined, assume a minimum of 4 feet for systems operating up to 1000 volts, and a minimum of 20 feet for systems operating above 1000 volts.

## Installation and Operation Requirements

**Extension Cords:** GFCI required with portable tools on extension cords (except where ground fault sensing breakers are already installed, or for exempted test equipment). No permanent use — temporary wiring only, per NEC article 590. Must be UL Listed.

**Adapter Cords:** Not used without a hot work/fire hazard permit; max length 3 feet. Owned/controlled by management of the hazardous area. Checked annually for ground continuity.

**Flexible Cords:** Permitted uses include pendants, light fixtures, portable lamps/signs/appliances, elevator cables, crane/hoist wiring, frequently-interchanged equipment, noise/vibration prevention, removable-for-maintenance appliances, data processing cables, moving parts, temporary wiring, and manufacturer-installed cords (NEC Art. 400.10). Not permitted as a substitute for fixed wiring, through walls/ceilings/floors, through doorways/windows, attached to building surfaces (except concealed), or in a manner exposing them to physical damage (NEC Art. 400.8). Always use cord/fittings identified for the application; never use as permanent wiring; replace (don't repair) damaged cord.

**Relocatable Power Tap (RPT / Power Strips):** Rated ≥15 amps for 120v circuits, indoor/dry use only. Must have resettable circuit breaker, on/off switch, surge suppression, UL listed/labeled, max 6 ft cord (unless management-approved). Plugged directly into a branch circuit receptacle — never into an extension cord or daisy-chained into another RPT. Extension cords must not be plugged into an RPT. Not permanently secured to structures/furniture. Cord not routed through walls/windows/doors/ceilings/floors or under carpeting. Permitted only in offices, labs, conference rooms, and computer workstations — not in maintenance areas, process areas, break rooms, kitchens, construction areas, hazardous/outdoor/damp locations.

- **May supply:** low-power lab equipment, computers/peripherals, telephones, low-wattage chargers, desktop staplers/pencil sharpeners/clock-radios, small personal fans, desk lamps.
- **May NOT supply:** appliances (refrigerators, coffee pots, space heaters, microwaves, toasters, crock-pots, grills, blenders, etc.), portable power tools, pedestal fans, copiers, laser printers, or any device drawing over 2.5 amps (up to 5 amps with management approval).

**Electrical Service Cords in Production Areas:** Max length 6 ft (single phase) / 12 ft (three phase) unless otherwise specified by area management (NEC 400.12). New outlet provided if long cord creates a safety/housekeeping issue. Cords checked before each use; damaged cords removed from service. Special-length cords require a reel/spool/hanger to keep excess cord off the floor. Explosion-proof plugs never permanently attached to non-explosion-proof equipment.

**Electric Portable Hand Tools and Other Devices:** 3-prong plugs required unless double insulated. Cords checked before each use. Portable heaters/radiant floor mats permitted only when issued by Lilly maintenance, and only UL-certified heaters with tip-over shutoff, automatic overheat shutoff, and cool-touch housing.

**Small Appliances:** Categorized as Heat Producing or Non-Heat Producing.
- Heat producing (coffee makers, refrigerators, microwaves, toasters) provided by the Company only — confined to designated break areas, on a PM schedule, plugged directly into an outlet (not an RPT). Exception: crockpots brought in with food, requiring an approved Fire Safety Appendix A submission and Maintenance electrical inspection with dated sticker. Prohibited permanently-installed appliances: toaster ovens/air fryers, George Foreman-style grills, griddles, convection ovens, coffee cup/candle warmers.
- Non-heat producing (pencil sharpeners, staplers, fans, radios) may be employee-brought if UL/FM-equivalent certified, defect-free, properly grounded, and regularly visually inspected.

Designated break areas must be common spaces (not personal), well ventilated around appliances, have ample dedicated outlets (no RPTs/power strips), solid-surface backsplashes, fixed permanent counters/cabinets, combustibles stored in closed cabinets, and be recognized by the building engineer/owner as a permanent space. Each IFM-managed building is inspected floor by floor annually per the Indiana Fire Code; noncompliant appliances are tracked on the building inspection report by the Lilly Site Fire Marshal, who issues an annual report to the distribution list.

**GFCI:** Used per NEC; tested at least annually per manufacturer's instructions.

**Electrical Identification:** Labeling requirements are specified for Medium Voltage Switchgear, Unit Substations (Load Centers), Motor Control Centers (MCC), Isolation Switches/Combination Starters/Adjustable Speed Drives, Lighting and Distribution Panels, Control Panels (panels installed after 1 Jan 2005 must show short circuit current rating), and Standby power sources. Distribution/Lighting Panel Upkeep Policy: panel schedules kept in a database owned by the LTC Electrical Engineer; two copies per panel; electricians update both copies by hand on change and route through the database editor.

**Location of Isolation Switches:** For routine lockout/tagout unrelated to electrical hazard, a disconnect switch must be located within sight and accessible (deviation may require a QEW to assist). For electrical-hazard-only lockout, a local or remote disconnect may be used per NEC.

**Warning Signs:** Must follow local rules/language. Rooms with exposed live parts >1000 volts: "DANGER – HIGH VOLTAGE – KEEP OUT". Outdoor substations with exposed live overhead equipment: "HARD HAT AREA".

## Safety Related Work Practices

**Required Engineering Studies:** Sites with their own distribution system maintain on file: fault current analysis, coordination study, and arc flash analysis (per NFPA 70E, completed/reviewed by a qualified electrical engineer from the Engineering Tech Center, which stores/manages the current electronic version).

**Training:**

| Group | Required Training |
|---|---|
| QEWs (Electricians, High-Voltage Electricians, Instrument Technicians, HVAC/Refrigeration Technicians, Electrical/Instrument Engineers) | 448188: KELEC1006 (Self-Study); HS20186 Low Voltage Electrical Safety Training for Affected Employees (Instructor Led); HS02045 QEW Contact Release (Self-Study); HS02043 QEW Audit – ESWC (OJT); one of HS02040/HS02041/HS02042 QEW Task Demonstration Assessment (Task Demonstration); trainers only: HS02044 TTT (Instructor Led). |
| Supervisors of QEW crafts | KELEC1006 (Self-Study); HS20186 Low Voltage Qualified Electrical Safety Training (Instructor Led). |
| Unqualified Affected Person (Maintenance Mechanics, Industrial Machine Operators, Production Operators, Machinists, Process Automation Engineers/Technicians, other Maintenance Supervisors) | 448188: KELEC1006 (Self-Study); HS20186 Low Voltage Electrical Safety Training for Affected Employees (Instructor Led). |
| Facility Operation/Maintenance and Engineering/Construction Management | 448188: KELEC1006 (Self-Study). |

Retraining: per NFPA 70E requirements.

**"Two Person Rule":** Supervisors review each energized work task to determine if two people are required. For AC circuits ≥600 volts, at least two qualified people must be present when working within Restricted/Prohibited Approach of exposed energized parts >1000 volts AC — exceptions for routine switching operations (excluding breaker racking/fuse changes) and emergency repairs to safeguard the public.

**Temporary Safety Grounding (>1000 volts AC):** Protects personnel from induced voltage/fault current, lightning strikes, switching failure/human error, and residual stored voltage. Practices: wear required PPE until grounds are in place; check for potential before grounding; use clamps with adequate capacity/strength; use only approved grounding equipment; install grounds as close to the work area as possible; connect jumpers grounded-end first then to phases (remove in reverse order, ground last); post a sign on grounded equipment until grounds are removed.

**PPE:** All QEWs must wear EH-rated safety shoes (not grounded safety shoes or grounding straps during electrical work) and non-conductive safety glasses (non-conductive glasses worn over conductive prescription eyewear). Must NOT wear metal jewelry during energized work.

**Elevated Work Platforms:** Comply with the Fall Protection Policy at minimum; additional requirements may apply for site-specific activities (e.g., bucket truck work on overhead lines).

**Lockout/Tagout for Electrical NALO Equipment:** Establishes requirements for lockout of electrical energy sources so conductors/circuit parts are disconnected, locked, and tested before work begins. Does not conflict with the general mechanical lockout procedure but addresses unique electrical control requirements. See USD-SOP-NALO Equip Lockout and Line Breaking Pgm-001.

> [!note]
> Philosophy: "When In Doubt – Lock It Out."

Preparation steps: review drawings/tags/labels/signs to locate disconnecting means; review other work activity for exposure risk; establish energy control methods for other hazardous energy sources; provide an adequately rated voltage detector; ground phase conductors/parts where induced voltage or stored energy is possible.

**Individual Employee Control Procedure:** Usable for plug-in equipment only, for minor maintenance on de-energized equipment where the disconnecting means is clearly visible to all involved employees and work doesn't extend beyond the shift — no lockout device placement required in this case.

**Sequence of Simple Lockout System Procedures:** (a) all personnel familiar with facility lockout/tagout requirements; (b) affected personnel notified, qualified person knows all disconnect/stored-energy locations; (c) de-energize/disconnect and relieve stored energy; (d) lockout all disconnecting means (or use a Hold card/tagout method plus one additional safety measure if no lockout means exists); (e) attempt to operate disconnect to verify lockout; (f)-(g) verify instrument operation and test for absence of voltage; (h) install grounding equipment where required; (i) equipment is now in an electrically safe work condition. Restoration: visually verify job complete, remove tools/materials and perform housekeeping, remove grounding equipment, notify personnel that lockout is complete/supply being restored, perform QC tests (e.g., Megger), remove personal lockout devices (only by the installer), notify equipment owner, return disconnecting means to normal.

**Complex Lockout:** Required when one or more of the following exist: multiple energy sources, crews/employees, crafts, locations, employers, unique disconnecting means, complex switching sequences, or work spanning more than one shift. Each area designates a person in charge who develops and communicates a written plan of execution.

**Electrical Manhole Entry:** Covered under the Confined Space Entry program (which governs in the event of conflict), for Visual Inspections, Short Term Observations, or Long Term Work. Entry does not occur when the enclosure contains standing water; only non-metallic ladders permitted if a free-standing ladder is needed. Departmental procedures with required equipment lists are maintained on file. All sources >1000 volts are de-energized prior to work (cable pulling/removal, splices, terminations) unless approved by electric shop supervision. Cutting an existing de-energized cable: tag the cable with circuit number and trace/mark it; if terminations have removable test caps, test with a voltage sensing device immediately prior to cutting; if not testable this way, all personnel are removed from the manhole and the cable is cut remotely from outside. Circuits need not be de-energized for manhole/cable inspections, cleanout, fire wrapping, or drain wire connections — electrician judgment applies. Attendant responsibilities (beyond confined space entry procedure): carry a Lilly hand-held radio on the site emergency channel; check/clear hazards around the manhole; verify clear egress space; block the opening from pedestrian/vehicle traffic with barricades/cones/tape. Training required for all personnel entering/attending manholes or signing the confined space entry permit, per an HSE-established frequency.

**Electrical Work in Hazardous (Classified) Areas:** NALO currently has no electrically hazardous areas; Corporate Electrical Safety Technical Requirements will be added if/when one is introduced.

**Energized Work:** ALL energized work requires proper PPE regardless of permit status. Use the Lilly Pre-Job Electrical Safety Form for work on/near exposed energized parts >50 Volts AC/DC. Energized work is approved by the Director (M3) or above for the area via the Energized Electrical Work Permit (Gensuite), except for: placing equipment in an electrically safe condition; replacing automation/control/instrumentation fuses; changing light bulbs; replacing plug-in lighting ballasts; testing/troubleshooting/voltage measurement; current testing with a clamp-on meter; infrared scanning or ultrasonic testing (without breaking the restricted approach boundary); visual inspections; and switchgear operation (racking breakers in/out, routine switching). Energized work must be performed by qualified personnel, with hazard elimination as the first priority.

**Electrical Switching Procedure (Load Center and Substation):** A written plan of action is required prior to any load center/substation switching, developed by the plant Electrical Engineer or a designated qualified electrician, communicated to all involved/affected personnel, and preceded by a review of the relevant one-line diagrams. Devices/systems must be fully checked for proper lockout (hold cards or lockout tags per Job Plan/MAP ESI instructions where applicable). Tagging/labeling accuracy must be confirmed and discrepancies corrected immediately. Unit/main substation switching is performed only by trained, designated Lilly electricians, with at least two electricians present (one performing, one observing) — a trained observer may substitute for the second electrician during emergencies. Switching electricians use two-way communication and wear approved PPE (leather protective gloves worn over dielectric gloves). Rubber gloves tested/replaced every 6 months; rubber blankets tested annually; hotsticks tested every 2 years. An approved voltage tester (checked before/after use) confirms absence of electricity before work. Barricades/cones/caution tape maintain a safe work zone. When operating a disconnect/breaker >1000 volts, the switching electrician notifies the electric/high voltage shop or site electrical engineer (or facilities manager if none exists) before and after switching, and notifies other affected maintenance groups; devices being opened are locked per lockout/hold card procedures.

**Electrical Emergency Response Procedures:** Reference the Emergency Response procedures for electrical emergency response information.
