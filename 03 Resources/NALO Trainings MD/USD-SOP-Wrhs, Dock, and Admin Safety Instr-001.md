# General Safety Instructions for Distribution Warehouses

*(Health and Safety Procedure)*

**Supersedes:** 001-002738, DOHS010
**Areas Involved:** North American Logistics Operations and support areas

## Purpose

To instruct all NALO employees (regular, summer, and contract) in general safety instructions for distribution warehouses.

## Introduction

NALO management/supervision at all levels are committed to an injury-free work environment — employee safety knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors are critical to that. Some topics here are covered in more depth in related safety training courses on employees' Learning Plans; every employee is responsible for knowing and following those courses' instructions.

## General Safety

All employees protect their own safety, co-workers' safety, and Lilly's physical assets, following the safety "NALO Organizational Structure and Responsibilities" per USD-SOP-HSE Mgmt System-001.

1. Don't perform unsafe acts or take risks to meet other priorities.
2. Flagrant/continuous disregard for safety instructions leads to disciplinary action.
3. Report suspected work-related injuries/illnesses to supervision and the Lilly-designated Health Care Provider immediately; report near misses to supervision immediately, per USD-SOP-Rptg Injuries Illnesses Incidents-001.
4. Notify supervision of any medical condition your work environment could aggravate (e.g., a sore back from activity at home or another job).
5. Report unsafe conditions immediately; route safety-improvement ideas to an area safety committee representative.
6. Report final-product breakage/spills to supervision immediately — clean up per USD-SOP-Spill Clean Up-001.
7. Report other spill types to supervision immediately — see USD-SOP-Plainfield ERP-001 and USD-SOP-Enfield and Fresno ERP-001.

## Personal Conduct

1. Ask your supervisor about any operation/procedure you don't fully understand.
2. Walk, don't run; use handrails on stairs and the three-point contact rule.
3. Don't climb on pallet racks, shelves, flow racks, powered equipment, or office chairs to reach something — use approved step stools/safety ladders.
4. No horseplay.
5. Don't throw objects.
6. Follow your area's food/drink guidelines.
7. Help familiarize new employees with hazards and safety practices.
8. Don't pass through an automatic door (overhead, sliding, swinging) until it's fully open and motionless.
9. Never leave an automatic door (especially overhead) partially open — raise/open fully.
10. Never step or walk on a pallet or pallet ring (slip/trip hazard).
11. Don't read/use cell phones, paperwork, or personal electronic devices while walking in the warehouse — cell phone use is only allowed in designated barricaded areas.
12. Don't wear headphones/portable devices in your ears while walking or working in the warehouse.

## General Emergency Response Information

Follow your area's emergency response plans and know the local emergency phone number (911 for PDC and EDC). Know the locations of: fire/evacuation rally points; tornado/severe weather shelters; Shelter-In-Place rally points; fire alarms; exits; fire extinguishers; safety showers/eyewash stations. Inform supervision/co-workers when leaving your work area, to help with Rally Point headcounts if needed. Avoid contact with blood/body fluids if a co-worker is injured — only trained personnel should clean that up.

## Material Handling

1. Use only NALO-issued utility knives/cutting tools.
2. Special hook knives are supplied for cutting straps/shrink-wrap.
3. Use only tweezers to clear a jammed tape machine (scissors at the machine are for cutting tape only); clean tape machine brushes regularly to help prevent jams.
4. Follow strapping guidelines per Health and Safety Training Course DOHS004_OJT.
5. Keep aisleways clear — don't store materials in designated aisles, and mark vehicle-travel aisles properly.
6. Use only pallets in good condition — inspect for intact stringers/solid slats.
7. Store pallets in designated locations, flat (not on end or side).
8. Empty wooden pallets in racks: max 14 high. Staged on the floor with direct sprinkler-head contact possible: max 18 high. *(Max height may vary by Distribution Center but never exceeds these limits.)*
9. Don't stack multiple empty pallets on a pallet pal; remove empty pallets before placing a new one (product, coolers, etc.); remove material from a palletized pallet pal one full layer at a time so the pal works as designed.
10. Don't stack more than one pallet of coolers/product on a pallet pal, given the added stack-height risk.
11. Plastic pallet stacking height: FM-approved (FM diamond marking) — 6 ft on floor or in rack; not FM-approved — 4 ft. *(Either type gets 6 ft if the rack location has a built-in sprinkler.)* All stacked plastic pallets (racked or floor) must be banded/shrink-wrapped. At Plainfield, stage plastic pallets in PLA5 for return to vendor.
12. Never manually handle more than one pallet at a time — prefer powered industrial trucks (PITs) for moving/stacking pallets.
13. If manual pallet stacking is required: 1 person — max 7 high; 2 people — max 10 high.
14. Don't drop pallets (causes damage/weakness).
15. Use leather palm or cut-resistant gloves for handling pallets, sharp/rough objects, and pulling used strapping from loads.
16. Stay alert for PITs — watch for warning lights, listen for horns, use safety mirrors.
17. Don't operate switches/buttons/powered equipment (dock levelers, doors, conveyors, elevators) unless trained on that equipment and the area is clear.
18. Only trained employees operate PITs; don't use any material handling equipment (powered or manual) without training on its safe use.
19. Safe PIT operation is primarily the operator's own responsibility — see USD-SOP-PIT Safety-001.
20. Use caution applying/removing rubber bands on pallets — consider a 2-person approach at times.
21. Keep pedestrian walkways clear of staged material/equipment, for pedestrian movement and unobstructed emergency-exit access.
22. Never wear headphones/personal speakers/electronic devices while operating a PIT.
23. Watch for and avoid trip hazards when removing strapping/stretch wrap from pallets.

## Ergonomics and Back Safety

1. Never manually handle more than one pallet at a time; use proper lifting technique/body mechanics; ask for help rather than lifting/pushing/pulling too-heavy loads — see training HS20003.
2. Bend knees, lift with legs, keep the load close to your body.
3. Keep shoulders straight over hips and move your feet to turn — avoid twisting while lifting/setting down.
4. Don't reach over an obstacle to lift a load — move it or go around.
5. Stretching (before, during, and after shift; morning stretches expected before shift) helps minimize muscle soreness/strain.
6. Participate in local Ergonomic program activities — all injuries are preventable.
7. Use good body mechanics; avoid awkward positions like pinch grip — lift cases with two hands, cupped underneath, not one hand (see Appendix B for examples).
8. Large coolers, or coolers/packages over 28 lbs, require a 2-person lift.

## Hazard Communication – Globally Harmonized System (GHS) & Labels

1. Safety Data Sheets (SDS) are available for materials NALO inventories — see training HS14031.
2. Ask supervision for a specific SDS, or find it on LillyNow.
3. Ask supervision about storage requirements/cautions for drugs, chemicals, or compounds.
4. Label formats vary but include basics like material name and flammability/corrosive properties.
5. Never remove labels from chemicals, compounds, raw materials, or company product.

## Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

1. Know the correct PPE for your tasks — see USD-SOP-PPE Assignments-001.
2. Ask supervision any PPE questions.
3. Understand your area's required safety-shoe use.
4. Know where to obtain PPE.
5. Inspect all PPE (including fall protection) before every use.

## Fire Safety

1. Know your work area's fire alarms, fire/emergency exits, and fire extinguisher locations.
2. Watch for fire hazards (loose wiring, worn/cut extension cords, missing extinguishers, open electrical panels, etc.).
3. Report fire hazards to supervision immediately.
4. Intervene if you see an outside truck driver, non-Lilly vendor, or contractor smoking on Lilly property.

## Dock Safety

1. Stay alert to dock traffic — congestion is a dock-area hazard.
2. Avoid unnecessary standing/walking through dock areas to minimize congestion/injury risk.
3. Remind/ensure drivers shut off trucks and set brakes when leaving the cab.
4. Use ladders/steps to move between trucks and docks — never jump off loading docks.
5. Don't operate dock levelers/locks without training — see USD-SOP-PIT Safety-001.
6. PIT operation in dock areas requires extreme care — see USD-SOP-PIT Safety-001.
7. *PDC only:* use caution near outbound dock doors 8–9 during active container loading/unloading — container swing radius may exceed expected limits; observe the floor markers for the container swing zone.

## Safety Permits and Tags

1. Attach "Do Not Use" tags to PITs taken out of service for repair (get tags from supervision) — see USD-SOP-PIT Safety-001.
2. Never attempt to start equipment with a Lockout lock/tag attached.
3. Watch for Hot Work Permits (HWP) posted in the work area, and for combustible-material accumulation nearby — never charge a PIT battery in an HWP-posted area (charging emits flammable gases).

*(See Appendix A for "Do Not Use"/"Lockout" tag examples.)*

## Housekeeping

1. Keep a clean, neat work area — clean up floor trash/objects that risk slips/trips/falls.
2. Store brooms, dust mops, and other cleaning supplies in cabinets or designated locations.
3. Remove/dispose of excess label backing material; don't let waste paper accumulate excessively.
4. Use extreme care with trash compactors — know their proper, safe operation.
5. Maintain a clear view when moving trash containers through the warehouse — get help if needed.

## Pedestrians

Pedestrians and PIT operators share responsibility for pedestrian safety.

**PIT operators should:** anticipate pedestrians suddenly entering the travel path; use the horn at intersections, doorways, aisleways, when backing, or overtaking pedestrians; drive down the middle of aisles where possible; after talking with a pedestrian, don't move until they've stepped clear.

**Pedestrians should:** read and follow posted warehouse signage; use designated pedestrian walkways (or main aisles if none are designated) as the primary travel path; stay out of designated "No Pedestrian" areas; stay out of narrow/wire-guided/swing-reach aisles when a PIT is present (rope off/barricade the ends if pedestrian presence is necessary); slow down, look, listen, and use safety mirrors/signals (PDC only) at intersections, blind corners, and doors opening into PIT paths; move conversations out of aisles; stay clear of moving/turning/backing PITs and their swing radius; never approach a PIT operator without first making eye contact/establishing communication; never stand/walk under a PIT's forks or raised load; never stand/ride on forks or catch a ride on a PIT; cross racking only at designated locations (never through "A" level locations); follow the 2-foot rule (keep 2 feet between yourself and a PIT) when approaching/communicating with operators.

## Lone Operators

For off-hours warehouse/maintenance work where an employee will be out of sight/hearing of others:
- Call Security and report you're working alone.
- Give your name, department, and expected work duration.
- Give your exact location.
- If using a PIT: have site security escort you into the warehouse (PDC also monitors via camera regardless of site).
- If not using a PIT: site security can monitor from the security office.

## Visitors

1. Unescorted visitors aren't allowed in the warehouse — the hosting Lilly employee is responsible for escorted visitors' safety/instruction.
2. Warn all visitors about PIT traffic and pedestrian rules.
3. Inform long-term visitors/contractors of emergency response procedures, Rally Points, tornado shelters, emergency phone numbers, etc.

## Non-Routine Tasks

Non-routine work is performed irregularly or for the first time, making its hazards harder to fully understand.

1. Supervisors should meet with employees before assigning/starting non-routine tasks.
2. Examples of non-routine work: infrequent tasks; a routine task performed at unusually high frequency; work outside normal duties; tasks with no documented procedure; tasks never performed before; routine tasks carrying high injury risk.
3. Before unplanned/non-routine work, consider: what injuries could occur? How severe? What causes/conditions/events would lead to them?
4. Supervisors use the Safe Work Permit (SWP) for Non-Routine Tasks in Gensuite.
