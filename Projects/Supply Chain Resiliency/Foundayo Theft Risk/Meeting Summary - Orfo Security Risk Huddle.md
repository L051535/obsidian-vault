### Purpose

The team reviewed **theft and security risk across the Orforglipron (Orfo) supply chain**, focusing on **transportation, third-party partners, and air vs. road movements**, to support an upcoming risk assessment discussion with leadership.

---

### Key Discussion Points

#### 1. **Overall Theft Risk Assessment**

- **Likelihood of theft is considered low overall**, especially for:
    - Bulk, palletized shipments
    - Movements already covered by existing security standards and audits
- Detection capability is strong: **any missing pallets or theft during transport would be noticed and reported** through existing metrics and Power BI reports.

---

#### 2. **Trucking vs. Airline Risk**

- **Trucking partners**:
    
    - Assessed against **Security Standard 200**
    - Covered by quality agreements, MSAs, audits, and assessments
    - Historical data shows **no theft issues**
    - Classified as **low risk**
- **Airline transport**:
    
    - Airlines are **not formally assessed**, and there is **no current airline-specific standard**
    - However:
        - Shipments are **palletized and loaded into ULDs (unit load devices)**, secured with netting
        - Theft of a full pallet would be obvious
    - Risk is still considered **low likelihood**, with sufficient **post-event detection**, even if mitigation is more reactive than proactive

---

#### 3. **Risk Increases as Product Breaks Down**

- Risk **increases when shipment size decreases**:
    - Pallets → cases → individual units
- In **Lilly Direct / downstream distribution**, where products are handled at the **unit or bottle level**:
    - Theft becomes **easier (“pilferable”)**
    - Risk assessed as **low-to-medium**, despite mitigations

---

#### 4. **Mitigations & Controls (Not Formal Slide Content)**

- Existing mitigations discussed (but intentionally **not added to slides**):
    - Anonymized labeling (e.g., no brand name on outer packaging, as done previously)
    - Freight forwarder requirements and subcontractor compliance
- Leadership (Noemi) requested **focus on likelihood classification**, not mitigations—though mitigations are expected to be the next question.

---

#### 5. **Geographic & Route Risk**

- Team agreed that:
    - **Route-level or geographic risk analysis is impractical**
    - Too many variables and possible paths
- Risk assessment remains **process- and control-based**, not route-based.

---

#### 6. **Risk Classification Alignment**

- Clarified that **High / Medium / Low ratings reflect likelihood**, not severity
- Consensus view:
    - Production & bulk transport: **Low**
    - Domestic trucking (outside Lilly walls): **Low, but slightly higher than production**
    - Lilly Direct / unit-level distribution: **Low–Medium**
- Risks are considered acceptable due to **multiple layered controls (“Swiss cheese” model)**.

### Bottom Line

- **No evidence of historical theft**
- **Controls are robust**, especially for bulk and transport stages
- Risk realistically increases only **late in the supply chain** when shipments are broken down
- Current framework supports a **low-to-low/medium risk narrative**, aligned with leadership expectations
