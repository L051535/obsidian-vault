## Executive Summary (Updated)

### Bottom Line

The team continues advancing the export capacity ramp initiative aimed at reaching **13 trucks/day capability**, but the discussion revealed that **material approval timing and shipment predictability remain the largest constraints**, not physical export capacity. Significant progress has been made in facility readiness, staffing, SOP improvements, and process design, but the group increasingly focused on establishing metrics and governance around approvals, schedule adherence, and inbound material flow.

## Key Discussion Themes

### What is Going Well

- Space creation and 5S activities are progressing as planned.
- Cross-training is expanding workforce flexibility.
- Export procedures and verification processes are being simplified.
- Transportation capacity appears capable of supporting future volume increases.
- Japan has approved shipment on U.S. pallets, reducing repalletization effort.
- Team believes the operation can perform at higher throughput levels when approved material is available.

### What Needs Attention

- Lack of consistent material approvals continues to create shipment instability.
- Team currently relies heavily on observations and judgments instead of defined KPIs.
- Forecasted truck demand and actual truck utilization are not always aligned.
- More structure is needed around inbound scheduling and material flow planning.
- Better visibility is needed into upstream quality approval processes and approval-related risks.

# Material Pipeline

## Overall Status: 🟡 Largest Constraint

This workstream drove most of the meeting discussion.

### Current Challenges

The team identified several issues impacting throughput:

- Large Japan and Korea batches frequently depend on late-stage approvals.
- Material availability does not consistently support steady daily truck flow.
- Schedule stability is affected by batch deviations discovered late in the process.
- Material approval performance is not formally tracked against expectations.

## Approval Timing Discussion

A significant discussion occurred between Export Operations and Customer Service regarding what "approved material" means and how success should be measured.

### Current Expectations

Customer Service explained that:

- Material should generally be approved approximately **three days before shipment**.
- Once a shipment reaches that threshold, unapproved batches should either:
    - Be escalated,
    - Be rescheduled,
    - Or be classified as special expedited cases.

### Key Observation

The team realized they lack measurable data showing:

- How often shipments meet approval targets.
- How often schedules are changed due to late approvals.
- How many "special expedited" shipments occur.

### Proposed Improvements

The group discussed:

- Creating approval-to-shipment KPIs.
- Tracking schedule adherence.
- Monitoring expedited shipments separately.
- Performing trend analysis over several weeks.
- Using data rather than anecdotal observations when discussing approval performance.

## Risk Management Discussion

A new topic emerged around managing approval risk.

### Suggestions Discussed

- Limiting the number of high-risk batches on any given shipping schedule.
- Establishing a manageable level of uncertainty within the plan.
- Defining how much schedule risk can be tolerated before changes are made.

The discussion emphasized creating a more predictable shipping plan while maintaining flexibility for critical products.
