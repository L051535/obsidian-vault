# Executive Summary

This session served as the official training and orientation for the 2026 LGDOS (Lilly Global Day of Service) MapSwipe project in partnership with the American Red Cross. The meeting was primarily intended for Area Coordinators (ACs) who will lead volunteer groups and cascade training materials to their teams.

The meeting covered:

1. LGDOS program context and coordinator responsibilities
2. Overview of MapSwipe and its humanitarian impact
3. How volunteers contribute to disaster response and humanitarian mapping efforts
4. Installation, account setup, and joining the Lilly user group
5. Walkthrough of MapSwipe projects and tutorials
6. Common troubleshooting questions
7. Expectations for Area Coordinators before September 24
8. Support resources and post-training communications

A major theme throughout the meeting was that MapSwipe provides an accessible, remote volunteering opportunity that can be completed from anywhere using a mobile device while contributing meaningful humanitarian mapping data used by disaster-response organizations worldwide.

## III. Introduction to MapSwipe and the Red Cross Partnership

### A. History of the Partnership

Jeff provided historical context:

- Partnership began during 2020
- Initial collaboration involved producing masks for veterans and nursing homes
- Current training represents the sixth year of collaboration

### B. Humanitarian Purpose

Jeff explained that MapSwipe supports humanitarian organizations by:

- Reviewing satellite imagery
- Identifying buildings and structures
- Supporting disaster response and humanitarian planning
- Providing critical mapping data in under-mapped regions

---

## IV. What MapSwipe Does

### A. Mapping Challenge

According to Jeff:

- Significant portions of the world remain inadequately mapped.
- Humanitarian organizations purchase satellite imagery but analysis remains expensive.
- Human volunteers help identify images containing structures worthy of professional review.

### B. Volunteer Impact

Participants learned that:

- Volunteers review imagery before specialists conduct detailed analysis.
- This significantly reduces analysis costs for humanitarian organizations.
- Each image is reviewed by multiple volunteers to increase reliability.

### C. Cumulative Lilly Contribution

Jeff shared that Lilly volunteers have collectively contributed substantial mapping efforts over previous years, covering geographic areas comparable in scale to a large U.S. state.

---

## V. Installing and Setting Up MapSwipe

### A. Device Requirements

Participants learned:

- Mobile phones and tablets are the preferred method.
- Laptop use is not recommended.
- Mobile experience is considered significantly easier.

### B. Account Creation

Participants were instructed to:

1. Download MapSwipe
2. Create an account
3. Select a username
4. Enter an email address
5. Create a password
6. Accept privacy terms

### C. Joining the Lilly Group

A key requirement was emphasized:

Participants must join the **Lilly User Group** within MapSwipe.

Purpose:

- Volunteer activity is attributed to Lilly
- LGDOS participation metrics can be tracked
- Organizational impact reporting becomes possible

---

## VI. Understanding MapSwipe Projects

### A. Project Availability

Participants were informed:

- Available projects change regularly
- Volunteers may choose any available project
- No assignment process is required

### B. Common Project Types

### Building Identification

Volunteers identify whether structures are present.

### Building Validation

Volunteers verify existing building outlines.

### Damage Assessment

Volunteers compare before-and-after imagery following disasters.

### C. Current Examples

Projects discussed included:

- Ecuador
- India flood recovery efforts
- Colombia disaster-response activities

---

## VII. Tutorial Demonstration

Jeff conducted a live walkthrough.

### Key Instructions

### Swipe Left

Used when no relevant structures exist.

### Green Selection

Used when a building is identified.

### Yellow Selection

Used when volunteer is unsure.

### Red Selection

Used when:

- Cloud cover blocks imagery
- Map data unavailable
- Image quality prevents review

### Important Reassurance

Participants were repeatedly told:

- Mistakes are expected
- Individual decisions are not final
- Multiple volunteers review every image
